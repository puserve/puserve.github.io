**Bekasi Regency, November 20, 2026 — A total of 24 President University students kicked off the “Sampah Tuntas” Thematic Community Service Program (KKNT) in Mekarmukti Village, Bekasi Regency, with an inaugural outreach session involving village officials and residents on Friday (November 20, 2026). The event, held at the Al-Fathoniyah Majelis Ta’lim, RT 001/RW 002, Hamlet 1, and starting at 2:15 p.m. WIB, marked the first step in a 12-week—or three-month—waste management mentoring program.**

To begin, the students introduced themselves and explained the purpose of their visit. They emphasized that they were not there to haul away trash or provide luxurious facilities, but rather to serve as mentors who would educate residents, especially mothers, on how to sort waste at home to prevent it from becoming mixed. During the presentation, the students introduced four categories of waste: organic, inorganic, hazardous and toxic materials (B3), and residual waste. Organic waste such as food scraps, vegetables, and bones can be processed into solid or liquid fertilizer using composters made from used water jugs, or utilized for maggot farming as an alternative fish feed. Inorganic waste, such as plastic, cardboard, intact glass, and cans, has economic value and can be deposited at the Waste Bank. The entire program is based on the 3R principles: reduce, reuse, and recycle.

“We have no intention of lecturing you. We are humbly asking for your cooperation\*.\* Over the next three months, we want to learn and work together with the community,” said Habil, PIC of the Mekarmukti Village KKNT Group.

Following the students’ presentation, the outreach session continued with input from local leaders, who provided an initial overview of the waste management issues on the ground. On that occasion, Mr Ratim, Head of Hamlet 1, explained that the native population of Mekarmukti is nearly 30,000, plus approximately 25,000 migrants. According to him, the shift in lifestyle from using leaf wrappings and cloth diapers in the 1990s to single-use plastics and disposable diapers has triggered a surge in waste. He noted that the village generates more than two to three metric tons of waste per day—too much for a single fleet to handle—while the cost of final disposal is quite high due to a pay-by-weight system and additional fees. Ratim also cited Bekasi Regency as one of the areas with the most severe waste problems in West Java.

Mr Ratim then detailed the areas most affected. The highest volume of trash is found in RT 01 and RT 02 (RW 01), a densely populated area with approximately 1,200 rental units in RT 02, generating one to three quintals per day. Trash often piles up along the road from the traffic light toward the cemetery, and the vacant lot behind the Komgas area remains a target for illegal dumping despite posted “No Dumping” signs. Disposable diapers were cited as the most difficult to manage, especially during the rainy season because they are slow to dry. He assessed that residents’ awareness remains below 50%, partly because the majority work in factories or offices and leave early in the morning and return in the evening. Therefore, he urged the students not only to provide technical education but also to motivate residents by understanding Bekasi’s local culture and maintaining the team’s reputation and cohesion.

From the perspective of waste management practitioners, Mr Yayat, the driving force behind the Mekarmukti Waste Bank (BSM) in RT 02/RW 01, supplemented this picture with a simple calculation. With 500 households, each producing an average of 1 kg of waste per day, a single RT generates half a metric ton of waste every day. Mr Yayat also shared his experience with a previous community service program (KKN) from Pelita Bangsa University, which involved educational outreach on sorting and processing plastic into paving blocks, an initiative that ran for several months before it was halted. The reason was that the wages for the sorting workers, about 80,000 rupiah per person per day, could not be covered by the proceeds from the sale of the processed materials. He hopes that students will not grow weary of conducting outreach so that residents’ awareness can continue to grow consistently.

This issue was further highlighted by BSM Secretary, Mr Irfan Fauzi. He explained that BSM has held a Village Government Decree since 2020 and is actually well-established in terms of both its system and facilities. Currently, the regular waste collection service is still operating with about 70 paying customers (a monthly fee of around Rp30,000), but waste management and recycling have once again come to a standstill due to a shortage of sorting personnel and a lack of public awareness. The root of the problem, said Mr Irfan, is that waste is not sorted at home, so it piles up in a mixed state. He also noted that the incinerator, which had been loaned during a previous Community Service Program (KKN), was ineffective because residents in densely populated areas complained about the smoke. Additionally, BSM frequently receives requests for the collection of Styrofoam and tissues from companies in the Jababeka area, which it cannot yet fulfill due to limitations in its processing technology.

“We must never offer or teach something we haven’t done ourselves. Leading by example is what matters most,” said Irfan. He hopes that President University students can develop technological breakthroughs that are more environmentally friendly and do not contribute to air pollution.

After hearing input from the speakers, the outreach session concluded with a technical discussion on implementation in the field. The students were divided into two groups: Group 1 was assigned to RW 01 with a base camp at the BSM headquarters, and Group 2 to RW 02 with a base camp at the Posyandu. Since RW 01 consists of four RTs, this group was further divided into sub-teams of six students, each responsible for two RTs. During the first week, the students will conduct door-to-door surveys and mapping to record waste sorting habits and estimate the weight of waste generated by each household. To maintain residents’ trust, students are prohibited from going out alone and must be accompanied by RT/RW officials. The groups were also asked to immediately submit their 12-week activity schedules to the BSM management and village officials.

Amid the guidance on ethics and appearance, the atmosphere briefly lightened. Students were asked to maintain proper decorum, avoid conflicts, and dress neatly in their university uniforms. Irfan then quipped, “Students are forbidden from falling in love okay!” which was met with laughter from the students and residents in attendance.

Stakeholders hope that collaboration among students, village officials, and residents can establish a sustainable, self-managed waste management system in Mekarmukti Village. A comprehensive evaluation during the second and third months will serve as a benchmark for the program’s success.

**Kabupaten Bekasi, 20 November 2026** — Sebanyak 24 mahasiswa President University mengawali program Kuliah Kerja Nyata Tematik (KKNT) "Sampah Tuntas" di Desa Mekarmukti, Kabupaten Bekasi, lewat sosialisasi perdana bersama perangkat desa dan warga pada Jumat (20/11/2026). Kegiatan yang digelar di Majelis Ta'lim Al-Fathoniyah, RT 001/RW 002, Dusun 1, dan dibuka pukul 14.15 WIB ini menjadi langkah pertama dari pendampingan pengelolaan sampah selama 12 pekan atau tiga bulan ke depan.

Sebagai pembuka, mahasiswa memperkenalkan diri sekaligus menjelaskan maksud kedatangan mereka. Mahasiswa menegaskan tidak datang untuk mengangkut sampah atau menyediakan fasilitas mewah, melainkan sebagai pendamping yang mengedukasi warga, terutama ibu-ibu, agar mampu memilah sampah dari rumah masing-masing sehingga tidak tercampur. Dalam paparan tersebut, mahasiswa mengenalkan empat kategori sampah: organik, anorganik, bahan berbahaya dan beracun (B3), serta residu. Sampah organik seperti sisa makanan, sayuran, dan tulang dapat diolah menjadi pupuk padat maupun cair melalui komposter dari galon bekas, atau dimanfaatkan untuk budi daya _maggot_ sebagai alternatif pakan ikan. Sampah anorganik seperti plastik, kardus, kaca utuh, dan kaleng memiliki nilai ekonomi dan dapat ditabung di Bank Sampah. Seluruh rangkaian program berpijak pada prinsip 3R: _reduce_, _reuse_, dan _recycle_.

"Kami datang bukan untuk menggurui, melainkan memohon kerja sama Bapak dan Ibu sekalian. Selama tiga bulan ke depan, kami ingin belajar dan bergerak bersama warga," ujar Habil, Ketua Kelompok KKNT Desa Mekarmukti.

Usai pemaparan mahasiswa, sosialisasi dilanjutkan dengan sesi masukan dari para tokoh setempat yang memberi gambaran awal tentang persoalan sampah di lapangan. Dalam kesempatan itu, Kepala Dusun 1, Bapak Ratim, menjelaskan bahwa penduduk asli Mekarmukti hampir mencapai 30.000 jiwa, ditambah sekitar 25.000 warga pendatang. Menurutnya, pergeseran gaya hidup dari pembungkus daun dan popok kain pada era 1990-an ke plastik sekali pakai dan popok instan memicu lonjakan sampah. Ia menyebut timbulan sampah desa mencapai lebih dari dua hingga tiga ton per hari sehingga tidak cukup diangkut satu armada, sementara biaya pembuangan akhir terbilang berat akibat sistem timbang berbayar dan retribusi tambahan. Ratim juga menyebut Kabupaten Bekasi sebagai salah satu daerah dengan persoalan sampah tertinggi di Jawa Barat.

Bapak Ratim kemudian merinci titik-titik yang paling terdampak. Volume sampah tertinggi berada di RT 01 dan RT 02 (RW 01), wilayah padat penduduk dengan sekitar 1.200 pintu kontrakan di RT 02, dengan timbulan satu hingga tiga kuintal per hari. Sampah kerap menumpuk di ruas jalan dari arah lampu merah menuju area pemakaman, dan lahan kosong di belakang area Komgas masih menjadi sasaran pembuangan liar meski sudah dipasang larangan. Popok sekali pakai disebut paling sulit ditangani, terutama saat musim hujan karena sulit kering. Ia menilai kesadaran warga masih di bawah 50%, antara lain karena mayoritas warga bekerja di pabrik atau kantor dan berangkat pagi serta pulang sore. Karena itu, ia berpesan agar mahasiswa tidak hanya memberi edukasi teknis, tetapi juga memotivasi warga dengan memahami kultur masyarakat Bekasi dan menjaga nama baik serta kekompakan tim.

Dari sisi pelaku pengelolaan sampah, penggerak Bank Sampah Mekarmukti (BSM) wilayah RT 02/RW 01, Bapak Yayat, melengkapi gambaran tersebut dengan perhitungan sederhana. Dengan 500 rumah yang masing-masing menghasilkan rata-rata 1 kg sampah per hari, satu RT menghasilkan setengah ton sampah setiap hari. Bapak Yayat juga mengungkap pengalaman KKN sebelumnya dari Universitas Pelita Bangsa, berupa penyuluhan pemilahan dan pengolahan plastik menjadi paving block, yang sempat berjalan beberapa bulan sebelum terhenti. Penyebabnya, upah tenaga pemilah sekitar Rp80.000 per orang per hari belum tertutup oleh hasil penjualan olahan. Ia berharap mahasiswa tidak bosan menyuluh agar kesadaran warga tumbuh secara konsisten.

Persoalan tersebut diperkuat oleh Sekretaris BSM Irfan Fauzi. Ia menjelaskan BSM telah memiliki SK Pemerintah Desa sejak 2020 dan secara sistem maupun fasilitas sebenarnya sudah mapan. Saat ini layanan pengangkutan reguler masih berjalan dengan sekitar 70 nasabah berbayar (iuran sekitar Rp30.000 per bulan), tetapi pengelolaan dan daur ulang kembali vakum karena minimnya tenaga pemilah dan belum tumbuhnya kesadaran warga. Akar persoalannya, kata Irfan, sampah belum terpilah dari rumah sehingga menumpuk dalam keadaan tercampur. Ia juga menyebut alat insinerator yang pernah dipinjamkan pada KKN sebelumnya tidak efektif karena asapnya dikeluhkan warga di kawasan padat penduduk, serta BSM kerap menerima permintaan pengangkutan styrofoam dan tisu dari perusahaan di kawasan Jababeka yang belum bisa dilayani karena keterbatasan teknologi pengolahan.

"Jangan sampai kita menawarkan atau mengedukasikan sesuatu yang belum pernah kita kerjakan sendiri. Keteladanan itu yang paling penting," ujar Bapak Irfan. Ia berharap mahasiswa President University dapat menghadirkan terobosan teknologi yang lebih ramah lingkungan dan tidak menimbulkan polusi udara.

Setelah mendengar masukan para narasumber, sosialisasi ditutup dengan pembahasan teknis pelaksanaan di lapangan. Mahasiswa dibagi dua kelompok: Kelompok 1 bertugas di RW 01 dengan _basecamp_ di markas BSM, dan Kelompok 2 di RW 02 dengan _basecamp_ Posyandu. Karena RW 01 memiliki empat RT, kelompok ini dipecah lagi menjadi sub-tim beranggotakan enam mahasiswa yang masing-masing memegang dua RT. Pada pekan pertama, mahasiswa akan melakukan survei dan pemetaan _door-to-door_ untuk mendata kebiasaan pemilahan serta perkiraan berat timbulan sampah tiap rumah. Demi menjaga kepercayaan warga, mahasiswa dilarang turun sendirian dan wajib didampingi perangkat RT/RW. Kelompok juga diminta segera menyerahkan jadwal kegiatan 12 pekan kepada pengurus BSM dan perangkat desa.

Di sela arahan soal etika dan penampilan, suasana sempat mencair. Mahasiswa diminta menjaga sopan santun, menghindari konflik, dan berpakaian rapi dengan almamater. Bapak Irfan pun menimpali dengan berkelakar, "Mahasiswa dilarang cinlok ya!", yang disambut tawa mahasiswa dan warga yang hadir.

Para pemangku kepentingan berharap kolaborasi mahasiswa, perangkat desa, dan warga dapat membangun sistem pengelolaan sampah mandiri yang berkelanjutan di Desa Mekarmukti. Evaluasi menyeluruh pada bulan kedua hingga ketiga akan menjadi tolok ukur keberhasilan program.
